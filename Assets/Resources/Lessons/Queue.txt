A **queue** adds items at the **rear** and removes them from the **front**. It follows **FIFO: First In, First Out**. The first item you add is the first one you remove.

> Think of a line at a ticket counter: people join at the back, and the person at the front is served first.

# Key operations
- **Enqueue**: add an item at the rear.
- **Dequeue**: remove the item at the front and return it. The next item becomes the new front.
- **Peek** (or Front): look at the front item without removing it.
- **IsEmpty**: check whether the queue has no items, so you never dequeue or peek an empty queue.

# Overflow and underflow
- **Overflow**: enqueuing into a queue that is already full.
- **Underflow**: dequeuing from a queue that is empty.

# Types of queues
- **Simple queue**: items join at the rear and leave from the front.
- **Circular queue**: the last position connects back to the first, so space freed at the front can be reused.
- **Priority queue**: items leave in order of priority instead of arrival order.

# Where queues are used
- Print jobs waiting for a printer.
- Keyboard input waiting to be processed.
- Tasks waiting for the CPU.

# Complexity
Enqueue, dequeue, peek and isEmpty all take **O(1)** time in a well-built queue. A queue holding n items uses **O(n)** space.

# Key takeaways
> - First in, first out: the oldest item leaves first.
> - Enqueue at the rear; dequeue from the front.
> - Every queue operation is O(1).
